# Video resume: script and shot list

Draft 1, 17 November 2026. For Theo to edit.

## Brief

| | |
|---|---|
| Length | 90 seconds. About 225 spoken words at a calm pace |
| Viewer | A founder or head of product at a startup or small tech company, deciding whether to book a call |
| Goal | They finish knowing three things: Theo leads product teams, he ships his own products, and he is easy to listen to |
| Where it plays | Homepage hero, Experience page, LinkedIn Featured |
| Format | 16:9 master at 1080p. A 9:16 cut of the first 30 seconds for phones |
| Tone | Plain and direct. First person. No buzzwords, no hype |

Rules that carry over from the site:

- Never say "open to work" or "looking for my next role".
- Do not name the current employer. Say "an HR software company".
- Use the softened figures: "eight-figure revenue impact", "a third fewer support contacts".
- Show marsHedge with sample data only. Do not show buyHCM or the Paychex support concept.

## Script

Six beats. Times are a guide. Read it aloud once before recording and cut any line you would not say to a person across a table.

### 1. Hook (0:00 to 0:10)

> I'm Theo Denanyoh. I lead product teams by day. On nights and weekends I design, build and launch my own software.
>
> Here is ninety seconds on both.

- **On screen:** Theo to camera, chest up, plain background.
- **Text:** "Theo Denanyoh" then "Product leader · Founder, KrumaLabs".

### 2. The day job (0:10 to 0:30)

> At an HR software company I lead three product teams and coach the senior product managers who run them.
>
> In two years our support and partner strategy had an eight-figure revenue impact, and customers needed to contact support a third less often.
>
> We also moved from shipping every two weeks to shipping twice a week.

- **On screen:** Start on Theo. Cut to three simple text cards, one per result, in the site's type and cobalt.
- **Text cards:** "Eight-figure revenue impact" · "A third fewer support contacts" · "Shipping twice a week".
- **Do not show:** any employer screen, dashboard or logo.

### 3. Why I still build (0:30 to 0:42)

> I started out building websites for local businesses, and I never stopped building.
>
> It keeps me honest. I know what a deadline costs the people doing the work.

- **On screen:** Theo to camera. One cutaway of hands on a keyboard with a code editor open on Cleanly.

### 4. The products (0:42 to 1:10)

> Cleanly is the one I'm proudest of. It runs a cleaning business from one place: scheduling, crews, quotes and payments. It's live on web, iPhone and Android, and I test it every week in the cleaning company I own.
>
> Tilly is a private chief of staff for your calendar. It's on the App Store.
>
> DirectoryHub lets anyone launch a directory site without code.
>
> I designed and built each of them.

- **On screen:** Screen recordings, Theo's voice over the top.
  - Cleanly: the web dashboard for 4 seconds, then the phone app completing a crew check-in for 4 seconds, then a wide shot of a finished room from TG Cleaning.
  - Tilly: the morning brief on a phone, one scroll.
  - DirectoryHub: creating a directory, then the published site.
- **Text:** product name and its address, lower left, for each.
- **Capture notes:** record at 60 frames a second. Use demo accounts with made-up names. Hide the browser bookmarks bar.

### 5. How I work (1:10 to 1:22)

> I think like an owner, I coach the people who run the products, and I stay close to the tools.
>
> That's what I bring to a team.

- **On screen:** Back to Theo. The three phrases appear as he says them.
- **Text:** "Think like an owner" · "Coach the PMs" · "Stay on the tools".

### 6. Close (1:22 to 1:30)

> The work and the full story are on this site. If you're building something, I'd like to hear about it.

- **On screen:** Theo to camera, a small smile, hold for one second after the last word.
- **End card:** "Td." mark, `theodenanyoh.com`, and "Get in touch".

**Word count:** about 225.

## Two ways to make it

### A. Synthesia avatar

- Use only a personal avatar made from Theo's own footage, with his cloned voice. A stock avatar does not meet the "looks exactly like me" bar.
- Beats 1, 3, 5 and 6 are avatar on camera. Beats 2 and 4 are text cards and screen recordings with the cloned voice over them.
- Check three things in the draft: how the voice says "Denanyoh", "Tilly" and "KrumaLabs"; whether the hands and blinks look natural in beat 1; and whether the smile at the end reads as real.
- Good for: fast edits when a product or a number changes.

### B. Self-recorded

- Record beats 1, 3, 5 and 6 to camera in one sitting. Record beats 2 and 4 as voice only.
- **Camera:** phone on a tripod at eye level, rear camera, 4K at 30 frames a second, landscape. Lock focus and exposure.
- **Light:** face a window. Turn off overhead lights.
- **Sound:** a clip-on or desk microphone within 30 cm. Record ten seconds of room silence for the edit.
- **Framing:** chest up, eyes a third of the way down the frame, a plain wall or a tidy desk behind.
- **Wardrobe:** a solid colour. The PackerBox tee from the current portrait works and is on brand.
- **Takes:** three of each beat. Pick the one where you sound like you are talking to one person.
- Good for: trust. Hiring managers respond to a real voice and face.

## How to compare the two

Watch both with the sound on, then with it off. Then ask two people who do not know the project one question: "Would you book a call with this person?"

Pick the self-recorded cut unless the avatar is indistinguishable. A slightly rough real video beats a slightly uncanny generated one for a hiring audience.

## After the pick

1. Upload the file to YouTube or Vimeo as unlisted.
2. Paste the link into Site Settings, under Video resume. The play buttons on the homepage and the Experience page appear on their own.
3. Add captions. Many people watch with the sound off.
